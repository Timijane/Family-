The World We Serve

The world is undergoing a profound demographic transformation — one that carries immense social, economic, humanitarian, and spiritual implications. For the first time in human history, the number of people aged 60 years and older is projected to surpass the number of children under five. Simultaneously, the largest generation of young people ever to exist is coming of age.

These two shifts — an ageing population and an unprecedented youth bulge — are reshaping societies. They are creating new pressures on families, healthcare systems, and social safety nets. And they are happening fastest in the countries least equipped to handle them.

This page is not about abstract statistics. It is about the real, measurable, and urgent crisis that PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION exists to respond to.

Section One

An Ageing World

The 60+ population is growing faster than any other age group. By 2050, one in five people on Earth will be over 60.

For the First Time in Human History

The world is undergoing a demographic transformation. For the first time in human history, people aged 60 and over are projected to surpass children under five. By 2030, 1.4 billion people will be 60+. By 2050, that rises to 2.1 billion — over one-fifth of the global population WHO .

What makes this shift urgent is where it happens. The WHO projects 80% of older persons will live in low- and middle-income countries — nations where elderly care systems are underdeveloped or absent.

The fastest-growing segment is those aged 80+. By 2050, 426 million people will belong to this group. They face the highest risk of disability, illness, and poverty.

Compassion in Action

Where the Burden Falls Heaviest

Most of the world's older population will live in countries least equipped to support them. Families — often already struggling — must bear the full weight of elder care without institutional support.

In Nigeria, the traditional extended family structure that once cared for the elderly is eroding under economic pressure and migration. Millions of seniors are left without adequate care.

In the United Kingdom, many older people face a different crisis: isolation and loneliness. Over one million older people in the UK report feeling lonely most or all of the time.

Section Two

The Next Generation

While the world ages, it is simultaneously experiencing the largest youth population in human history.

The Largest Youth Generation Ever

There are 1.3 billion adolescents aged 10–19 alive today — one-sixth of the global population. Including all young people under 25, the figure exceeds 3 billion — roughly 42% of humanity WHO .

Nearly 90% of adolescents live in low- and middle-income countries — regions where access to quality education, mentorship, and healthcare is severely limited.

The WHO reports that 1 in 7 adolescents experiences a mental health disorder. In most low-resource settings, mental health services for adolescents are virtually nonexistent.

The Silence Around Mental Health

In many communities — in Nigeria, the UK, and worldwide — mental health remains a taboo subject. Young people are told to "pray about it." Elderly people assume their despair is part of ageing.

The WHO reports that most adolescent mental health disorders go undetected and untreated — especially in low- and middle-income countries.

In the older generation, studies show 44.2% of older adults experience death anxiety, and over 92% report at least a moderate fear of death.

Section Four

Violence Against Women

One in three women. A crisis of global proportion that crosses every border, culture, and class.

Dignity & Safety

736 Million Women and Girls

The WHO estimates 1 in 3 women — approximately 30% globally — experience physical or sexual violence during their lifetime. That's 736 million women and girls WHO .

This is not merely a personal tragedy. It is a public health crisis. It crosses every border and class — but hits hardest where legal protections are weakest.

Prevention starts with reshaping attitudes early — teaching boys respect, responsibility, and empathy.

Section Five

The Addiction Crisis

296 million people used drugs in 2021. Young people are hit hardest.

Prevention

A Crisis That Begins in Adolescence

According to the UNODC, 296 million people used drugs in 2021 — a figure that has grown significantly UNODC .

Young people are disproportionately affected. Studies show the mean age of substance abuse initiation in Nigeria is as young as 12.4 years.

Without intervention, addiction derails education, destroys families, and perpetuates poverty. The most effective prevention is mentorship and self-worth.
