The Global Reality

An Ageing World That Cannot Be Ignored

The world is undergoing a profound demographic transformation — one that carries immense social, economic, and humanitarian implications. For the first time in human history, the number of people aged 60 years and older is projected to surpass the number of children under five. By 2030, 1.4 billion people will be aged 60 or over. By 2050, that figure will rise to 2.1 billion — more than one-fifth of the entire global population.

What makes this shift even more urgent is where these older people will live. The World Health Organization projects that 80% of older persons will reside in low- and middle-income countries by 2050 — nations where social safety nets, healthcare infrastructure, and elderly care systems are often underdeveloped or entirely absent. In these contexts, ageing is not accompanied by security; it is accompanied by vulnerability.

The fastest-growing segment is the oldest old — those aged 80 years and above. By 2050, 426 million people will belong to this group. They face the highest risk of disability, chronic illness, social isolation, and poverty. Without deliberate intervention, millions will spend their final years without dignity, care, or companionship.

Where the Burden Falls Heaviest

The overwhelming majority of the world's older population will live in countries least equipped to support them. This means families — often already struggling — must bear the full weight of elder care without institutional support.

In Nigeria alone, the traditional extended family structure that once cared for the elderly is eroding under economic pressure, urbanization, and migration. Millions of seniors are left without adequate care.

Why PHINEHAS GENERATION Is Responding

These numbers are not abstract. They represent grandmothers, grandfathers, widows, and retired community builders — people who have given their best years to society and now face isolation, untreated medical conditions, and poverty. PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION exists to restore dignity to these lives through medical outreach, food support, companionship, and compassionate care. We believe that honouring the elderly is not charity — it is a moral obligation.

The Future Is Young

The Largest Generation of Young People in History

While the world ages, it is simultaneously experiencing the largest youth population in human history. There are 1.3 billion adolescents aged 10–19 years alive today — approximately one-sixth of the global population. When expanded to include all young people under 25, the figure exceeds 3 billion — representing roughly 42% of humanity.

The geographic distribution of these young people mirrors the ageing crisis: nearly 90% of adolescents live in low- and middle-income countries. These are young people growing up in environments where access to quality education, mentorship, healthcare, and economic opportunity is severely limited. Without intervention, their potential remains untapped — and the consequences ripple across generations.

The World Health Organization reports that 1 in 7 adolescents experiences a mental health disorder. Depression, anxiety, and behavioural disorders are among the leading causes of illness and disability among young people. Yet, in most low-resource settings, mental health services for adolescents are virtually nonexistent.

A Demographic Snapshot of Global Youth

The scale of the youth population is unprecedented. More than 3 billion people are under the age of 25. Adolescents alone — those aged 10 to 19 — number 1.3 billion. The vast majority live in regions where opportunity is scarce, making targeted mentorship and education not just beneficial but essential.

The Urgent Issues

Global Challenges We Confront

Beyond demographics, there are pressing social crises that disproportionately affect the populations we serve. These statistics are not merely numbers — they are the reasons our programs exist.

Mental Health Crisis

Mental Health: A Silent Epidemic

280 million people worldwide live with depression. Among adults, 5% experience clinical depression globally. For adolescents, the figure is stark: 1 in 7 young people aged 10–19 experience a mental health disorder.

Most devastatingly, 727,000 people die by suicide every year — making it the third leading cause of death among 15–29-year-olds. The overwhelming majority of these deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries where mental health services are scarce or stigmatized.

Our Mental Health Awareness Program

PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION addresses this crisis directly through our Mental Health Awareness program. We educate teenagers on depression, emotional wellbeing, and resilience — creating safe spaces for conversation in schools and communities where the topic is often taboo. Early intervention saves lives.

Violence Against Women

The Scale of Gender-Based Violence

The World Health Organization estimates that 1 in 3 women — approximately 30% globally — experience physical or sexual violence during their lifetime. This translates to 736 million women and girls who have endured violence from an intimate partner or sexual violence from a non-partner.

Violence against women is not merely a personal tragedy; it is a public health crisis of epidemic proportions. It crosses every border, culture, and socioeconomic class — but its impact is most severe where legal protections are weakest and support services are unavailable.

Our "Real Men Don't Rape" Initiative

PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION confronts this crisis through our "Real Men Don't Rape" program — an educational campaign that promotes respect, responsibility, and gender-based violence prevention among teenage boys and young men. By reshaping attitudes early, we contribute to breaking the cycle of violence before it begins.

The Addiction Crisis

According to the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), 296 million people worldwide used drugs in 2021 — a figure that has grown significantly over the past decade. Of these, 39.5 million suffer from drug use disorders that require professional treatment.

Young people are disproportionately affected. Substance abuse often begins in adolescence, fueled by peer pressure, trauma, lack of guidance, and economic hopelessness. Without early intervention, addiction derails education, destroys families, and perpetuates cycles of poverty.

Prevention Through Mentorship

PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION's youth mentorship and Sexual Purity programs equip teenagers with the self-esteem, decision-making skills, and positive peer networks necessary to resist destructive behaviours. We believe that a guided teenager is far less likely to become a statistic.

Who We Are

About PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION

Founded in 2016 by Mrs. Oluwatosin Balogun Akano, PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION is a non-governmental organization committed to building stronger communities by empowering teenagers and caring for elderly people.

What started as a vision to mentor young people has grown into a movement of hope that now reaches families, communities, and senior citizens through education, advocacy, humanitarian outreach, and compassionate service.

From our beginnings in Abeokuta to our expansion into Lagos and the United Kingdom, our commitment remains the same: raising tomorrow's leaders while honouring yesterday's heroes.
