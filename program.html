Our Work

Every program we run is a direct response to a documented crisis. Where the WHO reports 1 in 7 adolescents face mental health disorders, we run mental health awareness. Where 80% of the world's elderly will live in countries least able to care for them, we run medical outreach and community welfare.

Each program runs annually and is designed to be repeated, measured, and expanded as resources allow. Together, they form a coordinated response to the two generations at the heart of our work — teenagers and the elderly, in Nigeria and the United Kingdom.

Below, each program is explained in full — what it does, where it runs, and who it serves.

01 Youth
Program 01 · Youth

Self-Esteem Development

Many teenagers grow up believing they are not enough — not smart enough, not worthy enough, not deserving of a future. Our Self-Esteem Development program exists to change that belief before it hardens into a lifetime of limitation.

We run interactive workshops in schools and community centres across Abeokuta, Ogun State. Teenagers learn to identify their own value, build confidence, set meaningful goals, and pursue them with conviction. The workshops are led by mentors, not lecturers — real people who listen, who share their own struggles, and who show teenagers that their futures are not written yet.

The goal is not just better self-esteem. It is character, purpose, and hope — the foundation on which every other life decision is built.

Program 02 · Prevention

Real Men Don't Rape

The WHO reports that 1 in 3 women experience physical or sexual violence during their lifetime. We believe the most effective prevention begins with teenage boys — before attitudes harden, before violence takes root.

Our "Real Men Don't Rape" campaign runs in schools and communities across Abeokuta. It teaches boys and young men that true strength is not force — it is respect, responsibility, and protection. The workshops challenge harmful norms, open honest conversations, and equip young men to be part of the solution.

We have held public walks against rape and gender-based violence, and we continue to work with schools to reach boys at the age when their character is being formed.

Program 03 · Youth

Sexual Purity & Relationships

Adolescence is the age when lifelong patterns are formed. Our Sexual Purity program reaches teenagers with honest, grounded teaching on relationships, sexual integrity, and responsible decision-making — conversations most young people never get to have.

We run relationship talks in schools, churches, and community venues, particularly around Abiola Way in Abeokuta. The sessions are direct, warm, and practical — combining moral guidance with real-life wisdom that helps teenagers navigate the pressures of their age.

Our approach is not shame-based. It is dignity-based — helping teenagers see their own worth and understand that the decisions they make now shape the adults they become.

Program 04 · Mental Health

Mental Health Awareness

The WHO reports that 1 in 7 adolescents experiences a mental health disorder — yet in most low-resource communities, mental health services are virtually nonexistent. Young people are told to "pray about it" or "get over it." That silence costs lives.

Our flagship mental health campaign is called "Depression! Not Me." It reaches teenagers with education on depression, emotional wellbeing, and resilience. We create safe spaces for honest conversation — in schools, in community halls, and in churches — where young people can talk about what they are really facing.

We are not clinicians. We are a bridge. When we encounter young people who need professional care, we connect them with the right services. And we work to dismantle the stigma that keeps so many silent.

Program 05 · Elderly

Medical Outreach

In the communities we serve, elderly people often go years without seeing a doctor — not because they don't need to, but because they cannot afford to. Small problems become permanent disability. Preventable blindness goes untreated. Dignity is quietly lost.

Our Medical Outreach brings healthcare directly to them. We conduct free eye examinations, fund cataract surgeries, provide prescription glasses, and administer medications and injections — all at no cost to the elderly person receiving care.

The program runs in Igbore, Abeokuta, and surrounding communities. Every outreach is coordinated with local leaders, so we reach the people who are most in need — the ones who would otherwise have no access at all.

Restoring a person's sight is not just medical care. It is independence, confidence, and hope returned.

Program 06 · Elderly

Community Welfare

Age should be honoured, not endured. Yet millions of elderly people spend their final years hungry, alone, and forgotten. Many have given their entire lives to their communities — and now the community has moved on.

Our Community Welfare program brings them back into the fold. We deliver food, financial support, and companionship to vulnerable seniors across Igbore and surrounding areas of Abeokuta. And we do it consistently — not once, but year after year.

The work is quiet. There are no cameras, no big announcements. There are just visits, conversations, and the simple human dignity of knowing that someone remembers you.

Because honouring the elderly is not charity. It is a moral obligation — and one we take seriously.

Program 07 · Widows

Widow Support

Widows occupy a uniquely vulnerable place in many Nigerian communities. Often they lose not just a husband, but also property, income, and social standing. In some cases, they are blamed, isolated, or left without any means of support.

Our Widow Support program exists to change that. We provide food support, financial assistance, companionship, and connection to community for widows in the areas we serve. We also walk alongside them through the practical challenges that follow loss — because grief is harder when you face it alone.

The scriptures are clear: "Religion that God our Father accepts as pure and faultless is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress." This program is our attempt to live that out, one widow at a time.

Program 08 · Volunteers

Leadership Training

Every volunteer who serves with PHINEHAS GENERATION FOUNDATION completes our Leadership Training — and for good reason. When you work with vulnerable teenagers and elderly people, good intentions are not enough. You need skills, ethics, and preparation.

Our Leadership Training is compulsory for every volunteer before they serve. It covers the foundations of ethical community work, safeguarding practices, trauma-informed engagement, and the character and integrity required to serve well.

We believe that leadership is not a title — it is the daily practice of serving others with excellence. Our training exists to build that practice into every person who wears the PHINEHAS name.
